\textbf{TL;DR}\\[0.3em]
The allocation-reduction effort has cut warm 2-file MTAC total allocation
\textbf{146.94\,GB $\rightarrow$ 58.10\,GB ($-60\%$)} and runtime
\textbf{101.95\,s $\rightarrow$ 91.30\,s ($-10\%$)}, with results bit-identical.
The two heavily-optimized phases (Main Search, Precursor Scoring) are no longer
dwarfing everything, which \emph{promotes} three previously-ignored phases ---
\textbf{Quantification \& Output, Protein Scoring, Spectral Library Loading} ---
to the top of the remaining list. The two largest reusable per-thread buffers
(fragment-index scratch, scored-PSM buffer) are \emph{already} reused across
files; they are not the remaining allocators. The best next targets are the
\textbf{serial $O(\text{library})$ map-builds} in Quant \& Output and Protein
Scoring (\(\sim\)3.9\,GB combined, single-threaded, so also a latency win).
}

\section{Methodology \& confidence}
Two independent measurements, used for different things:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Phase magnitudes} --- Pioneer's own per-phase accounting
  (\code{Mem Alloc (GB)} column), warm run (2nd \code{SearchDIA} in-process),
  2-file MTAC\_3P, 8 threads. This is the \emph{ground truth for magnitude}.
  \item \textbf{Within-phase line/function ranking} --- \code{Profile.Allocs}
  sampling profiler, single-thread, 1-file MTAC, sample rate $2\times10^{-4}$.
  Reliable for \emph{which} lines allocate; absolute GB trustworthy only for
  high-sample-count lines. Single-thread totals do not match the 8-thread phase
  table and must not be read as magnitudes.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Caveat (read before trusting any single line):} the sampling profiler's
top two leaf frames --- \code{process\_scans\_fused.jl:131} (92\,GB from \emph{7}
samples) and \code{scoring.jl:1459 select\_best\_per\_precursor!} (22\,GB from
\emph{1} sample) --- are low-sample, high-variance extrapolations. They correctly
\emph{point} at the per-scan fused deconv/score loop (Main Search) and best-PSM
selection (Precursor Scoring) as hotspots, but their GB figures are noise: the
phase table caps Precursor Scoring at 7.4\,GB total, so ``22\,GB'' cannot be real.
Magnitudes below are anchored to the phase table, not these extrapolations.

\textbf{Update --- Main Search resolved by deterministic bucketing.} The
\code{process\_scans\_fused.jl:131} mystery was settled with \code{gc\_bytes()}
sub-attribution (env \code{PIONEER\_DIAG\_MSLOOP}, single-thread, 1-file MTAC,
4{,}612{,}759 PSMs) --- see the Main Search subsection. The 92\,GB extrapolation
was an artifact; the real in-loop total is $\sim$3.8\,GB, dominated by one-time
buffer-growth churn, not the deconvolution.

\subsection*{Main Search [29.4 GB] --- sub-attributed via \code{gc\_bytes} bucketing}
Deterministic per-sub-step measurement of the fused scan loop (single-thread,
1-file MTAC, 4{,}612{,}759 PSMs). This replaces the unreliable 7-sample profiler
frame. In-loop total $\sim$3.8\,GB:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrll}
\toprule
\textbf{Sub-step} & \textbf{GB} & \textbf{Nature} & \textbf{Scales with} \\
\midrule
\code{score} (\code{score\_psms!}) & 2.752 & one-time buffer-growth churn & run (amortizes) \\
\code{df\_materialize}             & 0.778 & per-file output DataFrame     & files \\
\code{collect} (\code{sub\_indices}) & 0.204 & per-scan temp Vector \textbf{[FIXED]} & scans $\times$ files \\
\code{post\_dm}                    & 0.077 & per-scan, small               & scans \\
\code{prep}                        & 0.019 & per-scan, small               & scans \\
\code{run\_fused} / \code{dist} / \code{reset} & $\sim$0.001 & negligible & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\code{score} = 2.75\,GB is \code{growScoredPSMs!} block(500k)
  reallocation churn} --- copying the $\sim$46-field struct array as it grows
  $5\text{k}\rightarrow4.6\text{M}$ on the \emph{first} file (the $\sim$2.5$\times$
  copying overhead). Buffer capacity is retained across files, so this is
  \textbf{one-time per run} and amortizes to nothing on multi-file runs. Consistent
  with keeping block(500k).
  \item \textcolor{hi}{\textbf{\code{collect} = 0.20\,GB --- FIXED}} (\code{31d334c63}).
  \code{sub\_indices = collect(Int64, prec\_range)} allocated a fresh \code{Vector}
  every scan; \code{prec\_range} is already a \code{UnitRange\{Int64\}} and
  \code{run\_fused!} only iterates it, so it was pure waste. Now passed directly via
  type-assert. Per-scan $\Rightarrow$ scales with scans$\times$files ($\sim$4\,GB on
  a 20-file run); output byte-identical, PSM count unchanged.
  \item \textbf{The deconvolution itself barely allocates} (\code{run\_fused},
  \code{dist}, \code{post\_dm} $\approx$ 0). The loop is already lean --- the big
  numbers are one-time growth + unavoidable per-file output.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reusable-buffer status (answered)}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Buffer} & \textbf{Reused across files?} & \textbf{Mechanism} \\
\midrule
Frag-index emit scratch & \textcolor{hi}{\textbf{Yes}} & \code{SearchContext.frag\_index\_scratch}, grow-only \code{prepare!} \\
Per-thread counters/int bufs & \textcolor{hi}{\textbf{Yes}} & same \code{FragIndexScratch} \\
Scored-PSM buffer & \textcolor{hi}{\textbf{Yes}} & \code{SearchData.main\_search\_scored\_psms}; \\
 & & alloc once (5000), grow-only, capacity retained \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent Both large per-thread buffers live on the per-run \code{SearchContext}
and are grown once to a high-water-mark, then reused with zero further allocation
(Julia never shrinks array capacity on \code{resize!}-down). The residual
Main-Search allocation is \emph{not} these buffers --- it is the per-file
\emph{materialization} of PSM rows (the inner \code{vcat} that decouples the
result from the reused buffer before the next file overwrites it), which is
legitimate output, plus the per-scan work in the fused loop.

\section{Recommended next targets (priority order)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textcolor{hi}{\textbf{[DONE \code{31d334c63}] Main Search \code{collect}
  ($\sim$0.2\,GB/file).}} Per-scan \code{Vector} removed; bit-identical. The only
  clearly-wasteful per-scan allocation in the fused loop.
  \item \textbf{\code{build\_accession\_to\_species} ($\sim$2.3\,GB, Quant \& Output).}
  Serial, $O(\text{library})$, rebuilt at output time. Build once and cache on the
  spec-lib / search context, or restructure to avoid the per-call dict. Also a
  single-threaded latency win. \emph{One-time per run --- amortizes across files.}
  \item \textbf{\code{count\_protein\_peptides} ($\sim$1.6\,GB, Protein Scoring).}
  Same shape --- serial $O(\text{library})$ count. Precompute/reuse.
  \emph{Also one-time per run.}
\end{enumerate}
\noindent\textbf{Diminishing returns ahead.} With \code{collect} fixed, the Main
Search per-scan path is lean --- its remaining allocation is one-time buffer
growth (\code{score}, 2.75\,GB, amortizes) plus unavoidable per-file output
(\code{df\_materialize}, 0.78\,GB). The \code{StandardLibraryPrecursors}
constructor ($\sim$0.8\,GB) was investigated and found to be inherent one-time
Arrow string materialization over 6.8\,M precursors (the getters themselves do
\emph{not} allocate). So the remaining targets (2--3) are all $O(\text{library})$
\emph{one-time} costs that look large on 1--2 file profiling configs but amortize
on production multi-file runs. The allocation that genuinely scales with dataset
size --- the per-file Main Search loop --- has been brought to near-minimal.
